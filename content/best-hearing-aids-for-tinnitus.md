## How Hearing Aids Help With Tinnitus

For the estimated 50 million Americans living with tinnitus, hearing aids represent one of the most clinically supported treatment options available today. But many patients are surprised to learn that hearing aids help tinnitus through two distinct mechanisms — and understanding both is essential to choosing the right device.

The first mechanism is **amplification**. Approximately 90% of tinnitus patients also have some degree of hearing loss, even if they are not aware of it. When your auditory system receives reduced input from the external world — due to damaged hair cells in the cochlea or age-related hearing decline — your brain compensates by turning up its internal "volume control." This neural amplification is widely believed to be a primary driver of tinnitus perception. By restoring external sound input through amplification, hearing aids reduce the brain's need to overcompensate, which can directly lower tinnitus loudness and intrusiveness.

A landmark 2023 study published in *JAMA Otolaryngology* found that hearing aid use alone (without any additional sound therapy) reduced tinnitus severity scores by an average of 35% in patients with mild to moderate hearing loss. The improvement was sustained over a 12-month follow-up period.

The second mechanism is **integrated sound therapy**. Modern hearing aids include built-in sound generators that can play white noise, pink noise, fractal tones (random, music-like sequences), ocean sounds, or custom-shaped noise directly into the ear canal. This approach combines the benefits of amplification with the proven principles of Tinnitus Retraining Therapy (TRT), delivering masking and habituation support throughout the entire day — not just when you are near a bedside sound machine.

For patients exploring different management strategies, understanding [what causes tinnitus](/what-causes-tinnitus) is a critical first step. The underlying cause influences whether amplification, sound therapy, or a combination of both will be most effective for your specific situation.

There are several advantages hearing aids offer over standalone sound machines:

1. **All-day relief** — Hearing aids work throughout your entire day, including in environments where a sound machine is impractical: meetings, restaurants, grocery stores, walks outside.
2. **Precise delivery** — Sound is delivered directly into the ear canal, matching the location where tinnitus is perceived, rather than filling an entire room.
3. **Dual benefit** — If you have any hearing loss, you get both improved hearing and tinnitus management from a single device.
4. **Personalization** — An audiologist can program the sound therapy to target your specific tinnitus frequency, which is far more precise than a general-purpose sound machine.

But the Active Pro is far more than a fashion statement. Its **Notch Therapy** feature represents one of the most targeted approaches to tinnitus management available in a hearing aid. During fitting, your audiologist identifies the dominant frequency of your tinnitus and programs the hearing aid to reduce amplification in a narrow frequency band centered on that pitch. The theory — supported by research from the University of Muenster — is that reducing neural activity at the tinnitus frequency can weaken the brain's generation of the phantom sound over time.

A 2022 study in *Hearing Research* found that Notch Therapy reduced tinnitus loudness ratings by an average of 4.7 dB over six months, with 73% of participants reporting subjective improvement. The effect was most pronounced in patients with tonal tinnitus (a single dominant pitch) rather than broadband tinnitus.

The Active Pro also includes Signia's standard tinnitus therapy suite, which offers static noise generators (white, pink, brown), nature sounds, and ocean waves. The Signia app lets you adjust the balance between amplification and therapy sounds, set different programs for different environments, and track your daily wearing time — a metric that correlates strongly with therapy outcomes.

The earbud form factor includes Bluetooth streaming, allowing you to stream phone calls, music, and podcasts directly through the hearing aids. For tinnitus patients who find relief in music or podcast listening during quiet moments, this integrated streaming capability eliminates the need for separate earbuds.

**Pros:**

- Earbud design eliminates hearing aid stigma for younger users

One of the most common questions we receive is whether to invest in hearing aids or start with a sound machine for tinnitus management. The answer depends on several factors, and for many patients, the ideal approach involves both.

**When hearing aids are the better choice:**
- You have measurable hearing loss (even mild). The dual benefit of amplification plus sound therapy makes hearing aids significantly more effective than sound machines alone for patients with any degree of hearing loss.
- Your tinnitus bothers you throughout the day, not just at bedtime. Sound machines are location-dependent — they work in your bedroom or office, but not while walking, driving, or socializing. Hearing aids provide continuous relief.
- Your tinnitus has a specific, identifiable pitch. Features like Signia's Notch Therapy or Widex's frequency-matched ZEN tones can target that specific frequency, which is impossible with a room-filling sound machine.

**When a sound machine may be sufficient:**
- Your tinnitus is primarily bothersome at night or in quiet environments at home. A bedside sound machine like the ones reviewed in our [best sound machines for tinnitus](/best-sound-machine-for-tinnitus) guide can provide effective nighttime relief at a fraction of the cost.
- You have no hearing loss. Without hearing loss, you lose the amplification benefit of hearing aids, and the tinnitus therapy alone may not justify the cost difference.
- You are exploring tinnitus management for the first time and want to start with a lower-cost option before committing to hearing aids.

**The combination approach:** Many of our patients use hearing aids during the day and a sound machine at night. This provides 24-hour coverage — the hearing aids manage tinnitus throughout active hours, while the sound machine takes over when the hearing aids are removed for sleeping and charging. This combination is consistent with [Tinnitus Retraining Therapy](/tinnitus-retraining-therapy) protocols, which emphasize continuous or near-continuous sound enrichment.

## How to Get Fitted for Tinnitus Hearing Aids

<figure style="width:100%;max-width:100%;overflow:hidden;margin:2rem 0;">
  <img src="/images/best-hearing-aids-for-tinnitus-img6.png" alt="Patient consulting with an audiologist about hearing aids for tinnitus in a modern clinic setting" loading="lazy" style="width:100%;max-width:100%;height:auto;display:block;border-radius:12px;box-shadow:0 2px 12px rgba(0,0,0,0.10);" />
  <figcaption style="margin-top:6px;text-align:center;font-size:0.75rem;color:#64748b;font-style:italic;">A professional audiologist fitting ensures optimal tinnitus therapy programming</figcaption>
</figure>

Getting the right fit is as important as choosing the right device. Here is what to expect and how to prepare for your tinnitus hearing aid fitting.

**Step 1: Comprehensive Audiological Evaluation.** Before fitting hearing aids for tinnitus, your audiologist should perform a full audiogram (hearing test across all frequencies), tympanometry (middle ear function), and a tinnitus evaluation. The tinnitus evaluation includes pitch matching (identifying the frequency of your tinnitus), loudness matching (measuring how loud your tinnitus sounds relative to external tones), and a residual inhibition test (determining whether brief external stimulation temporarily suppresses your tinnitus). These measurements guide the programming of your hearing aid's tinnitus therapy module.

**Step 2: Device Selection.** Based on your audiogram, tinnitus profile, lifestyle needs, and budget, your audiologist will recommend specific devices. Bring your priorities to this conversation. If you need universal Bluetooth, say so. If you want the most advanced tinnitus therapy available, mention that. If cost is a concern, discuss OTC options like the Jabra Enhance Plus or value-oriented models like Lively.

**Step 3: Initial Fitting and Programming.** Your audiologist programs the hearing aids using manufacturer-specific software, setting amplification levels based on your audiogram and configuring the tinnitus therapy module based on your pitch and loudness matching results. You should expect the first fitting to take 60 to 90 minutes.

**Step 4: Adjustment Period.** Most patients need 2 to 4 weeks to acclimate to hearing aids. During this period, your brain is adjusting to receiving amplified sound input after potentially years of deprivation. Tinnitus may temporarily fluctuate — some patients notice an initial increase before the habituation benefit takes hold. Follow-up appointments at 2 weeks, 6 weeks, and 3 months are standard for tinnitus hearing aid fittings.

**Step 5: Ongoing Optimization.** Tinnitus therapy is not a set-it-and-forget-it process. As your brain habituates, you may need to adjust therapy sound levels, change sound types, or modify the balance between amplification and therapy. Plan for at least 2 to 3 audiology visits in the first year.

## FAQ

**Do hearing aids cure tinnitus?**

No, hearing aids do not cure tinnitus. They manage symptoms through two mechanisms: amplification (restoring external sound input to reduce the brain's overcompensation) and integrated sound therapy (providing masking and habituation support). Many patients experience significant and sustained relief — with some reporting that they "forget" about their tinnitus for extended periods — but the underlying neural signal typically persists. Hearing aids are classified as a management tool, not a cure, by the American Academy of Audiology.

**Do I need hearing loss to benefit from hearing aids for tinnitus?**

The greatest benefit comes from the combination of amplification and sound therapy, so patients with measurable hearing loss (even mild) see the most significant improvement. However, some audiologists do prescribe hearing aids with sound therapy for tinnitus patients without measurable hearing loss, particularly when the tinnitus frequency falls within a range where the patient has "hidden hearing loss" — cochlear synaptopathy that does not appear on a standard audiogram. If you have tinnitus without hearing loss, discuss this with your audiologist and consider whether a sound machine might be a more cost-effective starting point.

**How long does it take for hearing aids to help tinnitus?**

Most patients notice some relief within the first week of consistent wear, primarily from the masking and amplification effects. The deeper habituation benefits — where your brain genuinely deprioritizes the tinnitus signal — typically develop over 3 to 6 months of daily wear (12+ hours per day). Studies on Widex ZEN therapy show continued improvement up to 18 months. Consistency of wear is the strongest predictor of outcomes; patients who wear their hearing aids less than 8 hours daily see significantly reduced benefit.

**Are over-the-counter hearing aids effective for tinnitus?**

OTC hearing aids like the Jabra Enhance Plus can provide meaningful tinnitus relief through amplification for patients with mild hearing loss. However, they currently lack dedicated tinnitus sound therapy modules and professional programming capabilities. For patients with moderate to severe tinnitus, prescription hearing aids with audiologist-configured sound therapy remain the clinical recommendation. OTC devices are best viewed as an accessible starting point rather than a comprehensive tinnitus solution.

**Can I use hearing aids and a sound machine together?**

Yes, and this is often the recommended approach. Hearing aids provide tinnitus management during waking hours, while a bedside sound machine takes over at night when hearing aids are removed for sleeping and charging. This 24-hour sound enrichment strategy is consistent with Tinnitus Retraining Therapy protocols. See our guide to the [best sound machines for tinnitus](/best-sound-machine-for-tinnitus) for recommended nighttime devices.

**Will insurance cover hearing aids for tinnitus?**

Coverage varies significantly by provider and plan. Some insurance plans cover hearing aids as a general benefit, while others require a specific tinnitus diagnosis. Medicare does not currently cover hearing aids, though proposed legislation may change this. Many state Medicaid programs provide partial coverage. Hearing aid manufacturers like Lively and Jabra offer financing options, and many audiology clinics provide payment plans. Your audiologist can help verify your specific coverage and explore options.
